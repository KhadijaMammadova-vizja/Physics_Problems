## 3. Electrostatic Equilibrium

Find the equilibrium position for a charge \(q_3 = +1\,\text{C}\) placed on the line between a charge \(q_1 = +4\,\text{C}\) and a charge \(q_2 = +9\,\text{C}\), which are separated by a distance of \(2\,\text{m}\).

### Step 1: Set up the position
Let the equilibrium point be at a distance \(x\) from \(q_1\).  
Then its distance from \(q_2\) is

\[
2 - x
\]

Since all charges are positive, the forces on \(q_3\) are repulsive.  
For equilibrium, the two forces must have equal magnitude and opposite direction:

\[
F_{13} = F_{23}
\]

Using Coulomb’s law,

\[
k \frac{q_1 q_3}{x^2} = k \frac{q_2 q_3}{(2-x)^2}
\]

Cancel \(k\) and \(q_3\):

\[
\frac{4}{x^2} = \frac{9}{(2-x)^2}
\]

### Step 2: Solve the equation
Cross-multiply:

\[
4(2-x)^2 = 9x^2
\]

Take square roots:

\[
2(2-x) = 3x
\]

\[
4 - 2x = 3x
\]

\[
4 = 5x
\]

\[
x = 0.8\,\text{m}
\]

### Step 3: Interpret the result
So the equilibrium point is:

- \(0.8\,\text{m}\) from the \(+4\,\text{C}\) charge
- \(2 - 0.8 = 1.2\,\text{m}\) from the \(+9\,\text{C}\) charge

### Final Answer

\[
\boxed{x = 0.8\,\text{m from } q_1}
\]

or equivalently,

\[
\boxed{1.2\,\text{m from } q_2}
\]
