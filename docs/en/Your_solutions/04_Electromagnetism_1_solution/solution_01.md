## 1. Coulomb's Law

Four point charges of \(+1.0\,\text{C}\) each are placed at the corners of a square of side \(1.0\,\text{m}\). Find the magnitude and direction of the electric force on a charge of \(-2.0\,\text{C}\) placed at the center.

### Step 1: Distance from center to each corner
For a square of side \(a = 1.0\,\text{m}\), the distance from the center to a corner is

\[
r = \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{1.0}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.707\,\text{m}
\]

### Step 2: Force due to one corner charge
Using Coulomb’s law,

\[
F = k \frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}
\]

where

- \(k = 8.99 \times 10^9\,\text{N·m}^2/\text{C}^2\)
- \(q_1 = +1.0\,\text{C}\)
- \(q_2 = -2.0\,\text{C}\)
- \(r^2 = (0.707)^2 = 0.5\)

So the magnitude of the force from one corner is

\[
F_1 = 8.99 \times 10^9 \cdot \frac{(1.0)(2.0)}{0.5}
\]

\[
F_1 = 8.99 \times 10^9 \cdot 4
\]

\[
F_1 = 3.596 \times 10^{10}\,\text{N}
\]

This force is **attractive**, so each corner charge pulls the center charge toward that corner.

### Step 3: Net force from all four charges
The four forces have equal magnitude and are directed symmetrically toward the four corners of the square.

- Opposite corner forces cancel each other.
- Horizontal components cancel.
- Vertical components cancel.

Therefore, the vector sum is

\[
\vec{F}_{\text{net}} = 0
\]

### Final Answer

\[
\boxed{F_{\text{net}} = 0\,\text{N}}
\]

**Direction:** None, because the net force is zero.
