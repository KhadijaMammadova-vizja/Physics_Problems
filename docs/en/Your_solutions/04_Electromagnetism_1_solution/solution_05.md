## 5. Field Levitation

What electric field strength is required to make a proton levitate against Earth's gravity at the Earth's surface?

Given:

- Proton mass: $m_p \approx 1.67 \times 10^{-27}\,\text{kg}$
- Proton charge: $e \approx 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{C}$
- Gravitational acceleration: $g \approx 9.8\,\text{m/s}^2$

---

### Step 1: Condition for levitation

For the proton to levitate, the electric force must balance its weight:

$$
F_e = F_g
$$

$$
qE = mg
$$

So the required electric field is:

$$
E = \frac{mg}{q}
$$

---

### Step 2: Substitute the values

$$
E = \frac{(1.67 \times 10^{-27})(9.8)}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}}
$$

First calculate the numerator:

$$
1.67 \times 10^{-27} \times 9.8 = 1.64 \times 10^{-26}
$$

Now divide by the charge:

$$
E = \frac{1.64 \times 10^{-26}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}}
\approx 1.0 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{N/C}
$$

---

### Step 3: Direction of the field

A proton has positive charge, so the electric force is in the **same direction** as the electric field.

To balance gravity, the electric force must act **upward**.

Therefore, the electric field must point **upward**.

---

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{E \approx 1.0 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{N/C}}
$$

**Direction:** upward.
